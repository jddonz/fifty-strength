8-Week Strength + CrossFit Foundation

3 strength days/week · optional walking & mobility · knee-aware progression

Phase 1 - Learn the Movements

Weeks 1-2

Goals

  • Establish the 3-day training habit.
  • Learn the basic movement patterns.
  • Finish every workout feeling like you could do more.
  • Learn how your knee responds over the following 24-48 hours.

Rules

  • Strength effort: about RPE 5-6/10.
  • Full comfortable squat depth is allowed.
  • No sharp pain, catching, instability, or altered movement.
  • Do not train to failure.
  • Conditioning stays easy and controlled.

Starting Weight Method

  • First workout: choose a weight that feels deliberately easy.
  • Target about RPE 5-6: finish the set with roughly 4-5 good reps left.
  • If you could do 6+ more reps, increase slightly next set or workout.
  • If you could do only 2-3 more, reduce slightly.
  • Never sacrifice form to make the number work.

Phase 2 - Build Strength

Weeks 3-4

Goals

  • Move from exercise practice into consistent training.
  • Increase most movements to 3 sets.
  • Begin adding weight gradually.
  • Keep the knee response stable as volume increases.

Rules

  • Strength effort: RPE 6-7/10.
  • Usually finish with 3-4 good reps in reserve.
  • Add weight only when technique remains excellent.
  • Don't make up missed workouts.
Progression: If all reps are clean and you could do another 3-4 reps, add a small amount next time. If form deteriorates or the knee response is worse the next day, keep the same weight or reduce volume.

Progression Method

  • Work mostly around RPE 6-7: about 3-4 good reps left.
  • For rep ranges, use double progression: build reps to the top of the range before adding weight.
  • Example: 8/8/8 → 9/8/8 → 10/9/9 → 10/10/10 → add weight.
  • After adding weight, expect reps to fall back toward the lower end.

Knee: don't increase load and conditioning volume at the same time.

Phase 3 - Introduce the Barbell Squat

Weeks 5-6

Goals

  • Introduce light barbell back squats.
  • Build confidence with the barbell.
  • Increase upper-body strength.
  • Keep knee loading predictable and recoverable.

Rules

  • Barbell squat starts very light.
  • RPE roughly 6-7/10.
  • Full comfortable depth remains okay.
  • Wednesday is relatively knee-friendly.
Important: The purpose of the first barbell squat sessions is not to test strength. It is to learn how your knee responds to a new load and volume.

Barbell Starting Weight Method

  • Your first barbell squat is a calibration session, not a strength test.
  • Start with an empty bar or another very conservative load.
  • Build in small jumps until the working sets feel around RPE 6.
  • Stop adding weight once technique slows or you reach roughly 3-4 reps in reserve.
  • Keep the first few barbell sessions deliberately conservative so you can judge the delayed knee response.

Phase 4 - Start Becoming Strong

Weeks 7-8

Goals

  • Make the barbell squat a legitimate strength movement.
  • Continue building upper-body strength.
  • Introduce a real CrossFit-style metcon.
  • Finish the 8 weeks fitter and more capable—not beat up.

Rules

  • Strength effort: about RPE 7/10.
  • Still no maxing out or grinding reps.
  • Conditioning is challenging but controlled.
  • Keep monitoring the delayed knee response.
Target feeling: Finish a workout tired, breathing hard, and satisfied—but still feeling that you could have done another couple of minutes or a few more good reps.

Progression Method

  • Strength work should generally land around RPE 7: about 3 good reps left.
  • Add small amounts of weight only when all prescribed reps are clean.
  • Don't chase PRs during the 8-week foundation program.
  • For the Friday metcon, increase difficulty only if you can maintain good movement and recover normally afterward.

Notes & Reference

Read once, then check back as needed
Knee rule: The squat itself does not need to hurt to be useful. Track the delayed response the next morning. Mild, temporary ache is information; progressively worsening pain, swelling, instability, locking/catching, or limping is a reason to back off and consider evaluation.
Posture & upper-back work: Face pulls, wall angels, chin tucks, thoracic extension, doorway pec stretches, and push-up plus are built into every phase — one small posture movement per strength day, plus a cooldown stretch — so it becomes a normal part of training rather than a separate rehab routine. See the Optional Posture & Mobility Day at the bottom of each phase tab for the full, low-key version.
